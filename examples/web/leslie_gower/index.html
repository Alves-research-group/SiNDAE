Leslie-Gower predator-prey UDE

Solver: pounce, the solver for nonlinear programs, running in this page, with each model written by Pyomo in Python running in this page.

SiNDAE's Leslie-Gower example, examples/leslie_gower.py, trained simultaneously.

The ground truth

The Leslie-Gower predator-prey model of Bazykin (1998), as SiNDAE's LeslieGowerProblem writes it, with the prey x0 and the predator x1:

dx0dt=x0(r1−a1x1−b1x0)
dx1dt=x1z0,z0=r2−a2x1x0

The prey grows logistically and the predator eats the prey. The predator grows logistically too, with a carrying capacity proportional to the prey, which is the Leslie-Gower form. Step 1 solves this model by collocation from each initial condition and adds Gaussian noise of standard deviation σ0 and σ1 to every Nth point, which gives the measurements. The page starts from SiNDAE's values, r1 = 0.2, a1 = 0.2, b1 = 0.1, r2 = 0.2 and a2 = 0.01, from x0 = 1 and x1 = 0.1 over 0 ≤ t ≤ 80.

The trained system

The prey's equation stays as it is, and a neural network fNN(x, θ) of the states takes the place of z0, as eq. (3) of SiNDAE's Leslie-Gower notebook writes it:

dx0dt=x0(r1−a1x1−b1x0)
dx1dt=x1z^0,z^0=σzfNN(x−μxσx,θ)+μz

fNN is a multilayer perceptron with weights and biases θ, two inputs, hidden layers of 4 and 4 with softplus by default, and one linear output. The means μ and standard deviations σ come from the smoother's solution. The page starts the weights as SiNDAE's network does, uniform on ±1/√(inputs).

The Lyapunov option adds SiNDAE's constraint dV/dt ≤ 0 at every collocation time after the first, in the smoother and so in the training NLP, with V the ground truth's Lyapunov function about its equilibrium x0* = r1a2/(a1r2 + a2b1), x1* = r1r2/(a1r2 + a2b1):

V=ln(x0x0*)+x0*x0+a1x0*a2(ln(x1x1*)+x1*x1)

The training

Step 2 runs three things in turn. The smoother fits the states to the measurements with z0 free at every point and a penalty on (dz0/dt)2. The pretraining fits fNN to the smoother's (x, z0) by Adam. The simultaneous NLP of Lueg et al. (2025) then chooses θ with the states at every collocation point, starting from the smoother's solution, to minimize

minθ,x∑k12nk∑t∈Tk∑j=01(y~k,j(t)−y~k,jmeas(t))2
+λ‖θ‖2

over the trajectories k and the nk collocation times Tk of each, with each measured state interpolated linearly to those times as SiNDAE's _compute_norm_targets does, subject to the two ODEs written by collocation and to

z^0=σzfNN(x−μxσx,θ)+μz

at every collocation point. ỹ is each measured state standardized by the measurements' mean and standard deviation, and λ is the weight on the squares of the weights and biases. fNN's layers are written into the NLP as Pyomo expressions, so pounce treats θ as variables like the states. Step 3, the inference, fixes θ and solves the trained system from each initial condition, beside the ground truth.

Uncertainty quantification

The standard errors and the bands come from pounce's sensitivity of the solved NLPs, each derivative one backsolve with the KKT factorization of the solve. In the training NLP the measurements y are mutable Params, and in the inference NLP the weights θ are.

The trained weights

Cov(θ)=dθdyΣy(dθdy)T
Σy=diag(σj2)

with σj2 for each measurement of xj, the variance of the noise step 1 adds. Step 2's last row shows each weight and bias θi with ±2 SE(θi), where

SE(θi)=Cov(θ)ii

The trained states

Var(xj(t))=dxj(t)dyΣy(dxj(t)dy)T

at every collocation time, for x0, x1 and ẑ0, from the same derivatives with respect to y. Step 2's bands are

xj(t)±2Var(xj(t))

The inference

Var(xj(t))=dxj(t)dθCov(θ)(dxj(t)dθ)T

at every collocation time of each initial condition, with the training's Cov(θ). Step 3's bands are ±2 standard deviations the same way, and its table gives the share of the ground truth's points inside them.

Each is first order in the noise and holds fixed the means and standard deviations that standardize the data fit and the network. The bands show the spread the noise in the measurements causes, not the difference the network's form and λ cause between the trained model and the ground truth. The derivatives come from the KKT factorization of the training solve, so they may be inaccurate when the training uses L-BFGS in place of the exact Hessian.

Loading Pyodide, Pyomo and pounce.

The model and the method are SiNDAE's, by the Alves research group, github.com/Alves-research-group/SiNDAE, whose sindae package the page runs as it is. The page's own Python file, examples/web/leslie_gower/sindae_page.py, builds the network and pretrains it in NumPy where SiNDAE uses JAX, and gives SiNDAE's functions an NLP backend that solves each model with pounce's Python package for Pyodide through pyomo-pounce. The standard errors come from the KKT factorization of the training solve, so they may be inaccurate with L-BFGS, whose factorization holds L-BFGS's Hessian. The inference is SiNDAE's make_inference_model with the network written the same way. pounce starts it from the trained model integrated by the Runge-Kutta method of order 4, and where that integration's states pass 103, the inference ends at the last finite element before that time.
